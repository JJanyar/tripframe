Daytime footage is easy. The real test of a dashcam is a wet road at 11 p.m., with oncoming headlights and a dark car in front. Night video is where number plates become unreadable and where most people discover their camera is not as good as the box promised.

The good news: a few cheap habits improve night footage a lot, and you can check the result on your computer in minutes.

## Why night video is hard

A dashcam has a tiny lens and a small sensor. At night there is little light, so the camera raises the exposure time and amplifies the signal. That gives you two typical problems:

- **Grain and smearing.** Noise from amplification, and motion blur because the shutter stays open longer.
- **Blown-out lights.** Headlights and street lamps are far brighter than the dark road, so they turn into white blobs that hide detail around them.

A plate on a car with its lights on behind you is often the hardest case: the lamp next to the plate is blindingly bright compared with the plate itself.

## What helps most

**A good sensor.** Many cameras that are praised for night video use a low-light image sensor, such as Sony's STARVIS family, together with a wide aperture lens (a low f-number). This matters more than the headline resolution. A sharp 2K camera with a good sensor often beats a 4K camera with a weak one in the dark. See the [mid-range roundup](/blog/best-mid-range-dashcams/) for models that are known for this.

**HDR or WDR.** These modes combine a bright and a dark exposure so headlights do not wipe out the picture. It usually helps at dusk and in city lights. On some cameras it lowers the frame rate or adds a slight softness, so try it on and off with the same road and compare.

**A clean windscreen, inside and out.** Dirt and the film that builds up on the inside glass scatter oncoming light into streaks and haze. Wipe the inside of the glass around the camera now and then.

**Placement.** Mount the camera high, behind the mirror, so it sees through the cleanest part of the glass, usually inside the wiper sweep. Keep the lens as close to the glass as the mount allows. The [installation guide](/blog/how-to-install-a-dashcam/) covers where and how.

**No dashboard reflections.** A light-coloured dashboard or items on it reflect in the glass at night, especially when the car interior is lit by streetlights. A dark, matte dash cloth helps. A circular polarising filter, where your camera supports one, reduces reflections, but it also costs some light, so it helps most in the day and is a trade-off at night.

**Exposure settings, if your camera has them.** Some models let you adjust exposure compensation or turn HDR on and off. If the night picture is too bright and washed out, lower it a notch; if it is murky, raise it. Change one setting at a time and test.

## Test it before you need it

Do not wait for an incident to find out how your camera behaves.

1. Drive the same stretch of road once after dark, with a following car if you can, or stop and let a friend park a car behind yours.
2. Copy the clip to your computer.
3. Open it and pause on a moment with a car in front or behind. Can you read the plate at its normal distance?

Check this at several distances, not just when the car is close. A plate that is readable at 5 metres and gone at 15 is typical, and it tells you how much margin you have.

## Checking night clips in Tripframe

[Tripframe](/app/) is a free viewer that runs in your browser, so you can look at clips without installing anything or uploading them. For night footage it is useful for a few things:

- Step **one frame at a time**, because the sharpest frame in a clip is often not the one that appears when you pause. Motion blur varies from frame to frame.
- **Zoom** into the picture with the scroll wheel to see whether a plate is actually legible.
- **Save a still** as PNG, JPG or BMP of the best frame.
- Export just the relevant part of the clip, or find the loud moments, such as a horn or a bang, to jump straight to what happened.

Tripframe only shows what the camera recorded. It cannot make a dark clip brighter or change your camera's settings, so the fixes above have to happen in the car. See [all features](/features/) for the full list.

## If the footage matters

If a night clip is evidence, keep the original file untouched and work on copies. The steps for securing it are in [how to save dashcam footage for insurance or the police](/blog/save-dashcam-footage-for-insurance/). A blurry plate does not make a clip useless: the make and colour of the car, the road layout and the sequence of events are often what counts, and the car's lights, timestamp and GPS speed can support your account.

## Quick checklist

- Clean the glass inside and out.
- Mount behind the mirror, lens close to the glass.
- Use HDR/WDR if it helps in your test, not just because it is available.
- Reduce reflections from the dash.
- Test after dark, check the plates at several distances, and adjust.
