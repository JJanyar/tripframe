BlackVue cameras are known for their clean design, strong parking mode and cloud features. To watch the footage on a computer, BlackVue offers its own desktop program, **BlackVue Viewer**, for Windows and macOS. It works, but it is another program to install and keep up to date, and many people just want to look through last week's drives.

## How BlackVue names its files

`20240315_143022_NF.mp4`

- `20240315` is the date
- `143022` is the time
- The first letter after the time is the recording type: `N` normal, `E` event, `P` parking, `M` manual, plus a few more for impact and motion in parking mode
- The second letter is the camera: `F` front, `R` rear, `I` interior

The clips live in a single `Record` folder, so a full card is one long list of near-identical names.

## Watching BlackVue footage in your browser

[Tripframe](/) understands BlackVue names out of the box.

1. Put the microSD card in your computer.
2. Open [the app](/app/) in Chrome or Edge.
3. Choose the card or its `Record` folder.

Clips are grouped by day. Normal, event and parking recordings each get a label, so you can filter on events only and see straight away what triggered the G-sensor. Front and rear files from the same moment play side by side or picture in picture. Double-click the small view, or press `W`, to swap them.

### GPS

BlackVue writes its GPS log as NMEA data inside the file. Tripframe reads it and shows the route and speed on a map next to the video.

## When BlackVue Viewer is still the right tool

BlackVue Viewer can change camera settings, and the BlackVue app handles cloud features such as remote live view. Tripframe does neither: it is a player. For looking back at what happened, it is quicker, and it runs on any computer without installing anything.

## Keep your clips private

BlackVue owners often use the cloud. If you would rather keep certain clips off any server, Tripframe is a good fit: the files are read on your own computer and never uploaded.
