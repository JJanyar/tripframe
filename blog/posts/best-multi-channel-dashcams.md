A front and rear camera covers most incidents. But if you drive passengers, want proof of what happened inside the car, or worry about side impacts in a car park, a multi-channel dashcam is worth it.

Prices are rough street prices at the time of writing.

## Our picks

### Viofo A139 Pro 3CH and A229 3CH

Viofo's three-channel sets add an interior camera with infrared LEDs, so the cabin is visible at night. Image quality is excellent on all three. [How to view Viofo footage](/blog/viofo-dashcam-viewer/).

### Vantrue N4 Pro

Front and interior cameras in one unit plus a separate rear camera, at a good price. A long-time favourite among rideshare drivers. [How to view Vantrue footage](/blog/vantrue-dashcam-viewer/).

### Vantrue N5

Four channels from one camera: front, interior, rear and a fourth camera you place yourself. One of the few consumer four-channel dashcams.

### BlackVue DR770X Box

A different design: a small box under the seat with separate cameras on the glass. Discreet, built for fleets and taxis, and works with BlackVue's cloud. [How to view BlackVue footage](/blog/blackvue-dashcam-viewer/).

## Things to check before you buy

- **Interior infrared.** Without it, the cabin is black at night.
- **Total bitrate.** Three or four cameras write a lot of data. A 256 GB card is a sensible minimum; see [the best SD cards for dashcams](/blog/best-sd-cards-for-dashcams/).
- **Privacy.** Recording passengers can be regulated where you live. Tell passengers they are being recorded.
