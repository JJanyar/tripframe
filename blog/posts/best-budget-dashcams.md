A budget dashcam has one job: record every drive, reliably, in enough detail to show what happened. You do not need 4K, cloud features or radar parking mode for that. You do need a decent sensor, a camera that survives a hot summer, and a good memory card.

Prices below are rough street prices at the time of writing and move around a lot during sales.

## What to look for on a budget

- **A modern sensor.** Cameras with a Sony STARVIS or STARVIS 2 sensor handle night driving far better than no-name sensors.
- **1440p (2K) or better for the front camera.** 1080p is fine for showing what happened, but plates are easier to read at 1440p.
- **A supercapacitor instead of a battery.** Capacitors survive heat much better than the lithium batteries in cheap cameras.
- **GPS, if you want speed and location.** Often built into the mount or sold as an add-on.

### Viofo A119 Mini 2

The default recommendation for a single front camera. Small, with a STARVIS 2 sensor that does well at night, a supercapacitor and an optional GPS mount. It uses the same file naming as Viofo's bigger cameras, so [viewing the footage](/blog/viofo-dashcam-viewer/) is easy.

### 70mai M500

A small camera with built-in storage instead of a microSD slot, which removes the most common failure point. The trade-off: to get footage onto a computer you connect the camera with a cable or use the app. [How to view 70mai footage](/blog/70mai-dashcam-viewer/).

### Garmin Dash Cam Mini 3

About the size of a car key and nearly invisible behind the mirror. 1080p only, but Garmin's reliability and app are good. Note that Garmin files have no date in their name; see [how to view Garmin footage](/blog/garmin-dashcam-viewer/).

### Redtiger F7N

If you want front and rear on a small budget, the F7N family gives you a 4K front camera and a 1080p rear camera for about the price of a single-channel camera elsewhere. Night performance and build quality are a step below Viofo.

## What you give up

Compared with [mid-range cameras](/blog/best-mid-range-dashcams/), budget models usually have weaker night detail, no rear camera or a weaker one, and more basic parking mode. For most drivers, that is a fine trade.

## Do not skimp on the memory card

The cheapest part of a dashcam setup is the one that fails most. Use a high-endurance card: see [the best SD cards for dashcams](/blog/best-sd-cards-for-dashcams/).
